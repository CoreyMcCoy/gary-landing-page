What we do

Services

We work on houses. A visit starts with a diagnostic. That visit is $89 (example demo pricing) and we waive it if you approve the repair. We do not repair water heaters.

AC repair

Warm air, a condenser that hums and will not start, short-cycling, or a float switch that keeps shutting the system off. We test the capacitor and the contactor, check the blower motor amp draw, and look at the refrigerant circuit. Most older equipment here is R-410A. Many new systems use R-454B, a mildly flammable A2L refrigerant, and the tech follows the equipment maker’s procedure. A recharge is not the first guess. A system that is low has a leak.

Refrigerant recharge, when it is actually needed: usually $200 to $600, depending on type and amount. Example demo pricing.

Furnace and heat pump work

On a gas furnace we check the flame, the inducer, the limit, and the heat exchanger. If the heat exchanger is cracked, the furnace stays off. Combustion gases do not belong in the supply air. On a heat pump we check the reversing valve, the defrost cycle, and the auxiliary heat that has to carry a Central Texas freeze. No heat near freezing, or no cooling in dangerous heat, is an urgent 24/7 call.

A standard thermostat install is usually $150 to $300. Example demo pricing, confirmed before the work.

Tune-ups

One system, cooling or heating. We read the capacitor against its rated microfarads, inspect the contactor for pitting, measure blower amp draw, clear the condensate drain, wash a dirty outdoor coil if it needs it, and confirm the thermostat is calling. Gas heat gets a visual heat-exchanger inspection. A heat pump gets a defrost check. We do not top off refrigerant just because the truck is there.

$99 per system. Example demo pricing. Comfort Club includes two of these a year.

Duct cleaning

Supply and return trunks, plus the register boots we can reach. It is worth doing after construction dust, or when a return has been open to the attic and the far rooms are weak. It will not fix a failed capacitor, a dying blower motor, or a refrigerant leak. If the return is simply too small, cleaning will not resize it. We say which problem it is before anyone sells cleaning.

Quoted after we see the ducts. This demo has no flat price for duct cleaning.

Indoor air quality

MERV 8 is the filter we put in most 1-inch slots, changed about every one to three months. A MERV 11 or MERV 13 filter catches finer dust and it also raises static pressure. We step up only when the blower can handle it, often with a thicker media cabinet. Clammy summer air is usually a runtime problem: an oversized air conditioner short-cycles and never dehumidifies. A thicker filter will not fix that.

Filters, media cabinets, dehumidification, and fresh-air ventilation are quoted on site.

New installs

An air conditioner with a furnace, or a heat pump, sized from the house and the ducts rather than copied off the old nameplate. SEER2 is the current efficiency rating. A higher SEER2 can use less electricity under the test, and leaky ducts or a starved return can waste it. We say which layout fits after we see the fuel, the attic, and the rooms. The owner gives the quote.

Most full systems are about $7,000 to $14,000 after a free in-home estimate. Example demo pricing.

What a tune-up covers

  • Thermostat calls for heat and for cool, and the setpoint matches the room.
  • Filter size and condition. We say if a higher MERV rating is a bad idea for this blower.
  • Capacitor microfarad reading against the label on the part.
  • Contactor pitting, pull-in, and whether the contacts are welding closed.
  • Blower motor amp draw and a look at the wheel if airflow is weak.
  • Condensate drain, trap, and float switch.
  • Outdoor coil and condenser fan.
  • Temperature split. Refrigerant is discussed only if that check says the charge is off.
  • Gas furnace: flame, inducer, limit, and a visual heat exchanger inspection.
  • Heat pump: defrost cycle, reversing valve, and auxiliary heat.

Before the truck rolls

What helps the visit

  • If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the gas company. Do not wait for us, and do not use switches or flames.
  • Someone who can approve the repair should be home.
  • If you can safely read the model number on the outdoor unit, write it down.
  • Note the thermostat setpoint and what the room actually feels like.
  • Look at the return filter if it is easy to reach. A packed filter is a common reason for weak airflow.
  • Clear bins, toys, and tall grass away from the outdoor unit.
  • Have card, check, or cash ready. Payment is due when the job is done.

Nights, weekends, freezes, and heat waves

24/7 emergency service

Call (254) 555-0148. True emergencies are answered day and night. An after-hours or weekend emergency adds $125 (example demo pricing). A visit booked inside posted office hours does not add that fee.

Safety emergency — leave first

Gas smell, a carbon monoxide alarm, smoke or a burning smell from the unit, or sparking. Leave the home and call 911 or the gas company before you call us. Do not use switches or flames. Once you are outside, call (254) 555-0148 so the owner is alerted too. If anyone feels unwell, stay on the line with 911.

Urgent emergency

No heat when it is near freezing, or no air conditioning in high heat, especially with elderly people, babies, young children, someone sick, or pets at risk. Water pouring from the unit counts too. We treat these as urgent, 24/7. Expect a call from the owner within about 15 minutes. A clogged drain clear, when that is all it is, usually runs $120 to $200 (example demo pricing). The tech confirms the cause before the price.

Not an emergency

The system is running but weak, there is a new noise, the bill jumped, you want a tune-up, a quote, or a thermostat question. We book the next open appointment during office hours. Tell us the problem, your name, and the address. We will offer morning 8 to 11, or afternoon 12 to 3 or 2 to 5.

Do you fix water heaters?

No. We only do heating and air conditioning for houses. A water heater, a commercial job, or an address outside the usual area is not something we promise. Outside-area and commercial requests go to the owner to decide.

What refrigerant is in my system?

Many systems we see use R-410A. A lot of new equipment uses R-454B. R-454B is an A2L refrigerant, mildly flammable, so service follows the maker’s procedure. A recharge depends on the type and the amount, usually $200 to $600 (example demo pricing). We look for the leak instead of refilling a system that will be empty again next month.

What does SEER2 mean?

SEER2 is the current efficiency rating for new residential air conditioners and heat pumps. A higher number can mean less electricity for the same cooling under the test procedure that replaced the older SEER rating. The rating does not fix an undersized return, a crushed flex duct, or a blower that is already failing. We talk about those at the free in-home estimate.

Which MERV filter should I buy?

MERV 8 is the usual choice for a standard 1-inch slot, changed about every one to three months. MERV 11 and MERV 13 catch finer particles and they resist airflow. We only recommend the higher rating after a static-pressure check, or with a thicker media cabinet the blower can pull through. A filter change is not a repair for a bad capacitor or a low refrigerant charge.

How do I pay?
